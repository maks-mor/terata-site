The challenge

Most innovation value forms months or years before legal and financial systems recognise it as a verifiable asset.

Structured too late

Ownership, co-founder commitments and IP boundaries are typically settled only at legal incorporation or patent filing. By then, teams have often made informal equity promises and exposed unguarded technical know-how.

Arrow's information paradox

To be funded or evaluated, an idea must be shown; once shown without provable origin, exclusivity can leak. Without a pre-disclosure record, inventors withhold early work altogether.4

No comparable standard

Early IP and pre-incorporation ventures sit in dispersed drafts, spreadsheets and verbal pitches. Lack of a shared format keeps early-stage R&D illiquid and outside institutional deal flow.

The everyday failure mode in university labs and early teams

A researcher spots a commercial application inside an early experimental note, hesitates to share unpatented mechanics with mentors or transfer officers for fear of losing authorship control, and leaves the concept dormant in private files — where it never reaches capital markets.

Switzerland has the digital market rails

The legal framework, custody infrastructure and regulated trading venues for digital securities are operational. What stalls growth is upstream supply.

2021

Swiss DLT Act enters into force: ledger-based securities (Registerwertrechte, Art. 973d CO) from 1 February 2021; DLT trading rules from 1 August 2021.5

2022

SDX, F10 (now Tenity) and Aequitec complete the first tokenisation of private company shares inside a regulated CSD as dematerialised intermediated securities.6

2025

FINMA licenses BX Digital as Switzerland's first dedicated DLT trading facility under FinMIA.7

2026

FINMA approves merging SIX Digital Exchange into central depository SIX SIS alongside crypto custody.8

Why upstream asset supply remains bottlenecked

Paper-born companies are still retrofitted into digital shares manually one entity at a time. Meanwhile, post-hoc secondary patent marketplaces failed to scale — IPwe filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January 2024, later converted to Chapter 7 liquidation9 — because trading legacy patents without origin validation creates illiquidity.

Why origination must begin at day zero

With domestic Swiss investors supplying only 13% of later-stage scale-up funding in 20253, promising research IP frequently migrates before reaching institutional maturity. Establishing machine-readable structure, governance and provenance from the first draft bridges Swiss research and digital registries.

What Terata is

  • Software origination layer: turns fragmented concepts into structured project specifications prepared for legal review.
  • Provenance & boundary tool (planned): logs authorship chronology, with tamper-evident passport fingerprints planned in Phase 1 and Swiss/EEA private processing (Private Core) planned in Phase 2.
  • Pre-incorporation readiness filter: prepares comparable packages for TTOs, patent counsel, venture committees and digital share registries, making mentor and reviewer time more productive.

What Terata is not

  • Not a bank or securities depository: never holds client fiat funds, digital assets or regulated custody positions.
  • Not an exchange or trading facility: provides zero order matching, brokerage or secondary liquidity venues.
  • Not an issuer, adviser or patent filing: regulated issuance requires licensed partners, and a Terata passport does not create patent priority and is not a patent filing.

Aligned incentives, fully disclosed

Early-stage investing runs on an information gap: founders know more than investors and tend to under-report risk. Every Terata passport separates verified facts from open assumptions and lists open risks. In selected companies, and only where founders opt in, Terata may hold a small minority stake on standard terms, so that part of its return depends on the venture's long-term outcome rather than on fees alone. Because a stake can also bias judgment, the same risk standard applies whether or not Terata invests, every holding and its terms are disclosed to co-investors and partners, and Terata seeks no control rights that could block later rounds. A Terata stake is not a recommendation or investment advice.

For universities & technology transfer offices (strictly fee-only)

TTO and university workspaces are strictly fee-only: zero IP claims, zero equity, no right of first refusal (ROFR). Terata makes mentor and TTO review time more productive without taking equity in university spin-outs. Any optional minority stake occurs solely post-spin-out by separate founder opt-in on standard terms.

Conflict-of-interest policy (10 core rules)

  1. Equity is optional for founders and never a condition of service.
  2. One standard term sheet for all participating companies.
  3. Same risk standard with or without a stake; a founder's refusal is never shown to investors.
  4. Open risks are recorded before any equity discussion.
  5. Every holding (size, instrument, price, what it was for) is disclosed in the passport.
  6. No veto, no mandatory board seat, no success fee for fundraising.
  7. Stake instead of part of the fee — not on top of it.
  8. Founders are advised to take independent legal advice before agreeing.
  9. Outcomes of all companies with a Terata stake are published, including failures.
  10. Policy to be reviewed by Swiss counsel in Phase 1.
Standard passport disclosure line: Terata interest: [none / minority stake of X% via (instrument), received for (services/cash)]. Terata is not independent of this company.
